Equity dilution is expensive. Every time you raise a round, you give away a piece of your company — and that piece compounds with every subsequent round. Venture debt offers an alternative: growth capital that doesn't cost you ownership.

## What Is Venture Debt?

Venture debt is a type of debt financing provided to venture-backed companies. Unlike traditional bank loans, it's designed for startups and growth-stage companies that may not have positive cash flow yet but have strong fundamentals and institutional backing.

The key characteristics:

- **Term**: Typically 24–36 months
- **Interest rate**: Prime + a spread (usually 2–4% above prime)
- **Warrants**: Lenders typically receive warrants covering 5–20% of the loan amount
- **Repayment**: Usually interest-only for a period, followed by amortization

## When Does Venture Debt Make Sense?

Venture debt isn't right for every situation. It shines in these scenarios:

### 1. Extending Runway Between Equity Rounds

If you're 6 months from needing to raise but want to hit better metrics first, venture debt can bridge the gap. The cost of debt (interest + warrants) is almost always cheaper than the dilution from raising at a lower valuation.

### 2. Financing Specific Assets

Equipment, inventory, or receivables can be financed with debt rather than equity. If the asset generates revenue, it should pay for itself.

### 3. Accelerating Growth

If you've found product-market fit and the primary constraint is capital for growth (hiring, marketing, expansion), debt can fuel that growth without giving up more equity.

## What Lenders Look For

As someone who evaluates deals daily, here's what I focus on:

1. **Existing equity backing** — Most venture debt lenders require a prior equity round from a recognized investor
2. **Burn rate and runway** — How many months of runway does the debt add? We look for at least 12–18 months of extended runway
3. **Revenue trajectory** — Growing revenue (even if unprofitable) shows market traction
4. **Gross margins** — High-margin businesses can service debt more easily
5. **Capital efficiency** — How much revenue does each dollar of burn generate?

## The Deal Process

A typical venture debt deal takes 6–8 weeks from initial conversation to close:

1. **Sourcing** — Intro through network, portfolio companies, or direct outreach
2. **Credit evaluation** — Deep dive into financials, projections, and business model
3. **Term sheet** — Non-binding terms including loan amount, rate, warrants, and covenants
4. **Due diligence** — Legal, financial, and commercial DD
5. **Documentation** — Loan agreement, security agreements, warrant documents
6. **Closing** — Funding and disbursement

## Key Terms to Negotiate

Don't accept the first term sheet. Focus on:

- **Warrant coverage** — Negotiate down; every percentage point matters
- **Interest rate** — Even 0.5% matters over 3 years
- **Covenants** — Avoid financial covenants that trigger default too easily
- **Prepayment penalties** — Try to eliminate or minimize these
- **Material adverse change clauses** — These give lenders broad rights; narrow them

## Conclusion

Venture debt is a powerful tool, but it's not free money. It's a strategic instrument that, used correctly, can help you build a bigger company while keeping more of it. The key is understanding when debt is cheaper than equity — and that's almost always when you have clear visibility into revenue growth.

If you're a founder or CFO exploring debt to fuel growth, the best time to start the conversation is before you need the capital. Reach out early — the relationship matters as much as the terms.
