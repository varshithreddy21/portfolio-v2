India's financial landscape is undergoing a quiet revolution. Traditional bank lending, long the only game in town, is being complemented — and in some cases replaced — by private credit. For founders, this means more options. For investors, it means new opportunities. And for the broader economy, it means capital is flowing to where it's needed most.

## What Is Private Credit?

Private credit refers to debt financing provided by non-bank lenders — including alternative investment funds (AIFs), NBFCs, and family offices. Unlike traditional bank loans, private credit is:

- **More flexible** — Custom structures, tenors, and covenants tailored to the borrower
- **Faster** — Decisions in weeks, not months
- **Risk-tolerant** — Willing to finance companies that banks consider too early or too risky
- **Relationship-driven** — Lenders who take the time to understand the business

## Why Is Private Credit Growing in India?

Several forces are converging:

### 1. The Banking Gap

Indian banks are conservative by nature — and by regulation. Priority sector lending requirements, risk-weighted capital rules, and legacy NPA (non-performing asset) problems have made banks hesitant to lend to growth-stage companies. The gap between what businesses need and what banks will provide is enormous.

### 2. The Startup Ecosystem

India has over 100 unicorns and 100,000+ startups. These companies need growth capital, but many don't fit traditional bank lending criteria — they may be pre-profit, asset-light, or operating in new business models. Private credit fills this gap.

### 3. Regulatory Framework

SEBI's CAT-2 AIF framework has created a regulated structure for private credit funds. This gives investors confidence and gives fund managers a clear legal framework. The result: institutional capital is flowing into private credit at record rates.

### 4. Yield Hunger

In a low-interest-rate environment, institutional investors are hungry for yield. Private credit typically offers 12–18% returns in India, compared to 7–8% on government bonds. That spread is attractive enough to justify the additional risk.

## How CAT-2 AIFs Work

A Category II Alternative Investment Fund (CAT-2 AIF) is a SEBI-registered fund that can invest in a variety of instruments, including debt. Here's how the structure works:

1. **Fund sponsors** set up the AIF with a specific investment thesis
2. **Investors** (HNIs, family offices, institutions) commit capital to the fund
3. **The fund manager** identifies and executes debt deals with high-growth companies
4. **Returns** flow back to investors after the fund's management fee and carry

The key advantage: the fund can structure bespoke debt solutions — venture debt, mezzanine financing, structured credit — that banks can't or won't offer.

## What This Means for Founders

If you're building a high-growth company in India, private credit should be on your radar:

- **You don't need to be profitable** — But you need a clear path to profitability and strong unit economics
- **You don't need hard assets** — Many private credit deals are backed by cash flows, not collateral
- **You do need transparency** — The best deals go to founders who are open about their challenges
- **You should start the conversation early** — The relationship matters as much as the terms

## The Future

I believe private credit in India is still in its early innings. Here's what I see coming:

- **More specialization** — Funds focused on specific sectors (fintech, SaaS, climate) rather than generalist approaches
- **Secondary market** — Trading of private credit instruments, improving liquidity
- **Larger fund sizes** — As the asset class proves itself, more capital will flow in
- **Better data** — Standardized reporting and benchmarks will make the market more efficient

## Conclusion

Private credit isn't replacing bank lending — it's complementing it. For the right companies, it's the most efficient form of growth capital available in India today. The key is understanding when debt is the right tool, and finding a lender who understands your business.
